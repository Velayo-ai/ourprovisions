Which one, and why it’s judged against a bad photo

Households upload whatever they have. Someone will pick a bright kitchen, a sunlit lake, a screenshot chosen in ten seconds. The treatment has to be chosen for the worst photo, because it is applied to every photo and nobody will tune it per household.

What each one does

  • 1 looks best against a good photo and falls apart against this one — the column stops reading as floating and starts reading as pasted on. It also puts the busiest part of the screen right beside the thing you’re meant to read.
  • 2 keeps the photo legible, which is the argument for it: you can still see the counter, the water, the people. The cost is that detail still competes at the column’s edge, and a high-contrast photo will always fight the shadow.
  • 3 turns the photo into a field of its own colour and light. You lose the ability to make out faces, and you gain a background that cannot lose — the column reads cleanly against anything.

Recommendation: 3, with the blur kept modest. At 7px you can still tell a kitchen from a lake from a boat — the household still recognises its own photo, which is the entire emotional point — but nothing competes with the list. Much beyond about 10px and every photo becomes the same beige smear and you may as well not have one.

Two build details worth passing on

  • Blur the background layer, never the container — filter on a parent blurs its children. A positioned ::before or a dedicated div holding the image.
  • Scale that layer to about 1.06 and inset it a few pixels. A blurred element has soft, transparent edges, and without the overscan you get a pale halo around the whole window.

Not in scope

No new upload flow, no new setting, no per-household treatment. uploadHouseholdPhoto and updateHouseholdBanner already exist; desktop simply shows more of what the phone crops to a banner. Households with no photo fall back to the cream field — worth confirming that path renders, since it will be the common case for a new household.
